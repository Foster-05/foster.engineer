During the fall and spring of 2026 I had the chance to work with Professor
Leocadia Paliulis, from the Biology department at Bucknell, on yet another
previously completed Senior Design project.

This time, she described the situation to me as having worked well for a while,
but quickly fading in usefulness. The high-power microscope she uses to study
bats, spiders, and many types of biological samples has become increasingly
more difficult to use. Around a decade ago, an electrical engineering student
undertook the process of setting up better lights and fine motor control for
Paliulis to be able to control a glass rod finely enough to pop individual
molecules with precision. This system was running on a Raspberry Pi, and did
work, but was becoming very slow and unreliable. She wanted an update with something
a little more lightweight and usable long-term.

To that end, I decided to use an FTDI chip that would simply act as a translator
between USB and SPI, to cut out a microcontroller and much of the complexity
inherent in implementing one. The FTDI FT2232H series of devices are focused on
an MPSSE, or Multi-Protocol Synchronous Serial Engine, which is a software-controllable
form of interaction between many different types of serial communication. In addition
to the FTDI chip taking care of translation, I planned to integrate a system
with a large box and several power supplies into a few voltage regulators in
a small footprint, allowing for switching the voltage on one port for backlights
and for simultaneous outputs of multiple voltages for a fan and backlights.

This project is still in progress and will get updated soon. The current state
of the board may be seen in the images.
